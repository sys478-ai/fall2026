## Topic / Focus

This class consolidates Module 1 before we move into machines. Rather than introducing another major concept, we review the biological, psychological, social/political, and moral lenses by applying them to the same human phenomena and asking what each lens makes visible, what it leaves out, and what students now have a more stable method for doing when they encounter an unfamiliar AI system later in the semester.

_Format: Career ~45-55 min + integrated course ~45-55 min · Lesson template: Review + Synthesis_

There is no major new reading for this class. Students should bring notes from the first five class meetings and be ready to reuse them.

## Guiding Questions

- What has each Module 1 lens helped us see about human learning, judgment, and action?
- What does each lens leave out?
- Why is asking "Which lens is best?" usually the wrong question?
- How can these same habits prepare us to analyze AI systems in Module 2?

## In This Class

In this meeting, we will:

- complete the Career 2 activities on three possible lives and SMART goals
- run a review activity in which groups explain the same phenomenon through multiple lenses
- compare what biological, psychological, social/political, and moral explanations make newly visible
- launch the first synthesis homework as a bridge from Module 1 into Module 2

## Review Activity

Groups will choose or receive one ordinary example of learning, judgment, or behavior, such as learning to drive, becoming good at a sport, learning what counts as rude, falling for misinformation, or learning to use ChatGPT.

Each group will explain the same example four times:

- Biological: What does this lens notice?
- Psychological: What does this lens notice?
- Social / Political: What does this lens notice?
- Moral: What does this lens notice?

We will then compare:

- what each lens clarifies
- what each lens obscures
- which kinds of claims each lens supports most naturally

## Career / BRAID Integration

Career 2: Three Possible Lives + SMART Goals, paired with a Module 1 synthesis activity about different ways of explaining human learning, judgment, and action.
