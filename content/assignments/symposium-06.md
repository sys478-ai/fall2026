> ## Symposium Details
> The Fall 2026 Senior Year Seminar (SYS) Showcase will take place on **Tuesday, November 17, from 2:30–4:30 p.m.** in Alumni Hall at UNC Asheville. Attendance is mandatory.
> * **Hour 1:** Lightning-round talks or “elevator pitches” (1.5-2.5 min per project), cycling on stage by class/classes. Most classes will have 1-4 projects.
> * **Hour 2:** SYS students will table/showcase their work and discuss projects in more depth, with refreshments and hors d'oeuvres available. 

## Component 1: Research Poster

Your poster must:

1. Explain the AI system, including relevant technologies, people, and institutions.
2. Present a meaningful argument connecting the system to broader issues in AI and society.
3. Support substantive claims with credible evidence and citations.
4. Examine societal implications, including benefits, risks, and competing interests.
5. Consider important uncertainties, alternatives, or possibilities for change.
6. Include a references section with at least four credible sources.

The poster should be visually accessible and understandable on its own. Avoid large blocks of text, but do not sacrifice intellectual substance for visual appeal.

> **Purpose of the poster:** What is this system, how does it work, what does the research tell us, and why does it matter?


## Component 2: Interactive Visitor Experience

Your interactive element must:

1. Give visitors something meaningful to examine, compare, try, decide, or discuss.
2. Connect directly to the research and argument presented on your poster.
3. Help visitors explore a larger question about AI and society.
4. Be feasible to complete in approximately five minutes.

The interaction does not have to explain every aspect of your research. Visitors should be able to explore the poster for additional evidence, context, and analysis.


> **Purpose of the interaction:** How can we help visitors explore, question, or reconsider something important about this system? 
> * Your goal is to not only create an engaging experience about AI, but also to communicate a **research-supported argument about how AI systems interact with society** and invite visitors to think critically about it.


## Rubric

The final exhibit is worth **45 points**. Full credit means the criterion is met clearly and specifically. Partial credit means it is present but general, thin, or disconnected from the rest of the exhibit.

### Research Poster (20 points)

{: .table-simple}
| Criterion | Points | Full credit |
|---|---|---|
| **The system** | 4 | Explains what the technology does, how it works, and which people, organizations, and institutions build, deploy, and are affected by it. |
| **Central argument** | 4 | States a specific, arguable claim about how this system connects to broader issues in AI and society. Visitors can tell what the system is a case of. |
| **Evidence and citations** | 5 | Supports substantive claims with credible, independent sources. Includes at least four references. Separates what the evidence shows from what is value judgment. |
| **Societal implications** | 4 | Identifies who benefits and who bears risk. Represents competing interests and perspectives fairly. |
| **Uncertainty and alternatives** | 3 | Names what is unknown or contested. Considers design choices, safeguards, or policies that could change outcomes. |

### Interactive Visitor Experience (15 points)

{: .table-simple}
| Criterion | Points | Full credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaningful activity** | 5 | Visitors examine, compare, try, decide, or discuss something concrete. The activity is more than entertainment. |
| **Connection to research** | 5 | The activity connects directly to the poster's argument and evidence. Visitors can see where to learn more on the poster. |
| **Larger question** | 3 | Helps visitors explore or reconsider a larger question about AI and society, not only the system itself. |
| **Feasibility** | 2 | Works in about five minutes. Materials are ready, and the team can facilitate it smoothly. |

### Communication and Teamwork (10 points)

{: .table-simple}
| Criterion | Points | Full credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual clarity** | 3 | The poster is readable on its own, with clear headings and purposeful visuals. Text is concise without losing substance. |
| **Lightning talk** | 3 | Presents the system and argument clearly within the time limit. |
| **Visitor discussion** | 4 | Team members answer questions with evidence, acknowledge uncertainty, and engage visitors thoughtfully. All members contribute. |
