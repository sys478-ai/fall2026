**Part 4 of 5** on the way to the AI & Society Symposium (Nov. 17). Team deliverable, 10 points.

## What You're Doing

Translate your research into something a visitor can understand and engage with in approximately **five minutes**. Design it for the audience you chose in your proposal.

In Part 3, you developed an argument about how your AI system works, the choices and priorities that shape it, and its consequences. Now your task is to **turn that argument into a visitor experience**. What should visitors see, do, or discover to understand your central argument?

## Guidelines
Your exhibit should include two complementary elements:

1. **A visual display** (such as a poster, trifold, or digital display) that communicates your central argument and the four exhibit sections: What You See, What's Underneath, What's at Stake, and What Could Be Different.

2. **An interactive element** that invites visitors to investigate, experience, or reflect on an important idea from your research. This might be a physical activity, a simple digital interaction, or a guided choice or discussion.

These elements should work together to communicate a coherent argument. Visitors should be able to learn from the visual display on its own, while the interactive element offers an opportunity to engage more deeply.

## What To Submit

1. **An exhibit storyboard** showing what visitors will see, do, and learn.
2. **A working or low-fidelity prototype** of your interactive element.
3. **Draft explanations** of each of the four exhibit sections:
   - **What You See:** an accessible introduction to the system, its purpose, and how people encounter it.
   - **What's Underneath:** how it works, the design choices and priorities embedded in it, and whose interests shape it.
   - **What's at Stake:** evidence of its societal impacts, including your case study, who is affected, and what remains uncertain.
   - **What Could Be Different:** alternative designs, policies, or approaches, including who has the power to make changes.
4. **A plan for how you will communicate your evidence and recommendations** to visitors.

Submit one package per team on Canvas by the due date shown above.

### Tips
- The interactive element should make a visitor **decide something** or **notice something**, then connect it to your evidence.

  Your exhibit should tell a coherent story, not simply present four separate collections of facts. Each section should help visitors understand your central argument.
- Your exhibit should also help visitors understand something larger than the particular AI system you're studying. Think about how your case illustrates a broader issue or question about technology and society. What should visitors take away that they could apply to other technologies or situations?
- If you use images, music, or other people's work, make sure you have the right to.
- Every team member must be prepared to explain the research and answer questions.

## Next Class

During the next class, you will test your interactive experience with classmates who have not seen it. Watch what they do, not just what they say. Be ready to walk someone through the exhibit and find out whether its main message is coming across.
