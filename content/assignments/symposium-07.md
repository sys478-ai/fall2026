Submit an individual reflection (approximately 400–500 words) addressing the following:

**Part 1: Reflecting on the Symposium**

1. **What did you learn?** What was the most important or surprising thing you learned while researching your symposium topic? How did your understanding of the technology or its societal implications change?
2. **Communicating with others:** What did you learn from explaining your project to visitors or classmates, and hearing their questions and perspectives? Did anything challenge or expand your thinking?

**Part 2: From Critique to Possibility (from [Tuesday's in-class activity](../meetings/28))**

3. **Human needs and values:** What needs, interests, and tensions did your symposium team identify?
4. **Alternative approaches:** Describe at least **one alternative technology and one low-tech or non-technological solution** that could support these same needs or values. What might each make possible?
5. **Your vision:** Complete the following statements based on your own thinking and the group discussions:
   - **The future I envision:** I want a world in which…
   - **What could make it possible:** This might require…
   - **Where we could begin:** One concrete step could be…
6. **What you learned from others:** What new idea or perspective emerged from the mixed-group discussion? Did it change or expand your thinking in any way?

Your reflection doesn't need to be a polished essay. Focus on explaining your ideas, what you learned from others, and how your thinking developed through these experiences.

If you choose to use GenAI to edit your answer, briefly explain how you used it and what it helped you accomplish.
