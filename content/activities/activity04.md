## Case #1. BidShift: A Digital Platform for Filling Extra Nursing Shifts

### The Problem

Hospitals need enough qualified nurses to cover shifts, including nights, weekends, and holidays. Unfilled shifts can increase overtime costs, strain staff, and affect patient care.

### The Proposed Solution: BidShift

In July 2026, UNC Hospitals announced **BidShift**, a digital platform for filling extra nursing shifts.

Nurses could view available shifts and submit bids indicating the additional premium pay they would accept, above their regular compensation. Qualified nurses offering to work for lower premiums would generally receive preference, subject to staffing needs.

The goal was to fill shifts while managing labor costs.

### Group Discussion

1. **Problem definition:** What problem is BidShift trying to solve? Are there other ways to understand the underlying problem?
2. **Assumptions and incentives:** What assumptions does the system make about nurses and staffing? What behaviors does it encourage?
3. **Power and consequences:** Who benefits, who might bear the costs, and who gets to establish the rules?
4. **Broader significance:** What is this a case of? How does it relate to technological solutionism?


### Addendum: What Happened Next?

In August 2026, nurses raised objections to the bidding arrangement. UNC Hospitals subsequently announced it would **not use the competitive bidding feature**, instead retaining the scheduling platform without competitive pay bidding.

**Final reflection:** What does this outcome suggest about whether technologies – and the priorities embedded in them – are inevitable?

## Case 2. Deconstructing a Technological Solution – AI Workplace Monitoring


### The Problem

A company providing legal services wants to ensure that employees deliver consistent, high-quality customer service. Employees regularly speak with clients about sensitive matters, including wills and probate.

### The Technological Solution

In 2026, Co-op Legal Services in the UK was reported to be using an AI system to evaluate employee phone calls.

The system assesses interactions against more than 50 measures and generates scores used in employee performance evaluations. The company describes the technology as a way to support service quality and employee development.

### Group Discussion

1. **Problem definition:** What problem is this technology trying to solve? Are there other ways to understand the underlying problem?
2. **Assumptions and measurement:** What assumptions does the system make about good customer service? What might be difficult to measure?
3. **Power and consequences:** Who benefits, who might bear the costs, and who gets to define good performance?
4. **Broader significance:** What is this a case of? How does it relate to technological solutionism and workplace surveillance?
