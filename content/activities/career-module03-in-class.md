## Career Module 3: Life After College

This in-class activity asks you to connect career ideas with financial realities. You will estimate cost of living, research salary ranges, and consider how this information affects your post-graduation plans.

It also introduces salary negotiation as an important part of career planning. Most students will begin below the median salary for a field and work upward over time, but starting salary still matters because it shapes later raises and long-term earnings.

### 1. Cost Of Living Reality Check

Open [NC Reality Check](https://www.cfnc.org/plan-your-future/plan-for-a-career/reality-check/) and complete the activity based on the lifestyle you anticipate for your first year after college. If you plan to live outside North Carolina, the [NerdWallet cost of living calculator](https://www.nerdwallet.com/cost-of-living-calculator) can help compare locations.

<!-- .compact -->

- What expenses did the tool ask about that you had not thought about before?
- Which costs seem higher or lower than expected?
- Which choices felt realistic for your first year after graduation?

### 2. Connect Budget To Occupations

Use the **Find Occupations** option to explore jobs connected to your estimated budget. You can also search for occupations connected to your possible lives from the previous module.

<!-- .compact -->

- How do your lifestyle expectations compare with the income connected to your possible career paths?
- What information from the tool was useful?
- What information still feels incomplete?

### 3. Salary Research

Use at least two of these tools to research a possible career path:

<!-- .compact -->

- [CareerOneStop Salary Finder](https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Wages/compare-salaries.aspx)
- [Glassdoor Salaries](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/index.htm)
- [Monster Salary Tool](https://www.monster.com/salary/)
- [ZipRecruiter Salaries](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries)
- [Indeed Salaries](https://www.indeed.com/career/salaries)
- [O\*NET Online](https://www.onetonline.org/)
- [Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/survey/)

Record what you find. If possible, compare by:

<!-- .compact -->

- job title
- location
- entry-level versus experienced roles
- required education or credentials
- industry or organization type

### 4. Salary Negotiation Basics

Salary negotiation is easiest when it is grounded in solid information. Three kinds of information matter especially:

<!-- .compact -->

1. what you need to live
1. what the field typically pays
1. what strengths, skills, and accomplishments you bring to the position

Your budget helps you identify the salary range you could realistically accept. The low end of that range is your "walk away" number. The high end is your stretch goal. In many cases, it makes sense to think in terms of a range rather than one fixed number.

Today, we are focusing on the first two parts of that process: budget and salary research. In the next module, we will build the third part by identifying the strengths, skills, and accomplishments you can use to support a salary conversation.
