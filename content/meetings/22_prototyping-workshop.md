As noted in the [final assignment](../assignments/symposium-06), your exhibit has two required components:

1. **A research poster** that explains your system, develops your argument, presents evidence, and connects your topic to broader societal issues. The poster provides the intellectual foundation.

2. **An interactive element** that invites visitors to engage with your research by exploring a question, examining evidence, making a decision, or considering a trade-off.  The interactive element helps visitors engage with your ideas.


{% collapsible closed %}
## 1. What Makes a Meaningful Exhibit?

**Discussion:** What's the difference between an interesting activity and an exhibit that helps visitors understand an important issue?

Consider two possible exhibits about **fake news and AI-generated deepfakes**:

- **Exhibit A: Can You Spot the Fake?** Visitors examine several news stories, images, and videos and try to guess which are real and which are AI-generated.

- **Exhibit B: Would You Share This?** Visitors encounter a fabricated breaking-news post with an AI-generated video and a high share count. They decide whether to share, verify, or ignore it. After learning the story is fake, they examine research on why people believe and share misinformation, and consider whether individual fact-checking is enough to address the problem.

**Three design principles:**

1. **Show, don't just tell.** Give visitors something concrete to examine, compare, try, or decide.
2. **Connect the experience to a larger issue.** The activity should reveal something about the relationship between technology and society.
3. **Ground your claims in evidence.** Your exhibit should communicate research-supported arguments, acknowledge uncertainty, and represent relevant competing perspectives.

## 2. Design Your Research Poster (25 minutes)

{:.mt-4}
Before designing your exhibit, clarify the intellectual substance of your research. This is the job of the poster. Your poster should communicate your research and analysis to visitors, even when no one is standing beside it to explain.

Create a rough layout for your poster using paper, slides, or another design tool. I have created a [sample poster template](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1Zg3LkCfYKGYxootHXaB-JoBCbKqrErgH/edit?usp=sharing&ouid=113376576186080604800&rtpof=true&sd=true) (that needs to be jazzed up) that you are welcome to use.

Your poster must address five areas:

**1. The System**
- Why does this system exist? What benefits does it provide?
- What does the technology do, and how does it work?
- What people, organizations, and institutions are involved?
- What technical or organizational choices shape the system?

**2. The Central Argument**
- What broader issue about AI and society does this system illuminate?
- What should visitors understand or reconsider?

**3. Evidence and Research**
- What research findings, data, examples, or case studies support your argument?
- What evidence is especially important for visitors to see?
- What claims remain uncertain or contested?

**4. Societal Implications**
- Who benefits, and who faces risks or disadvantages?
- What social, economic, political, or ethical issues arise?
- What competing perspectives or values should visitors consider?

**5. Questions and Possibilities**
- What choices could change how the system operates or affects society?
- What alternatives, safeguards, or approaches are worth considering?
- What larger questions remain unresolved?

**References:** Include citations for substantive claims and a references section with at least four credible sources. Draw on independent research and reporting, not just company websites or promotional materials.

Your poster does not need to follow a rigid academic format. Use diagrams, visual evidence, concise explanations, and clear headings to make the research accessible to a general audience.

**Deliverable:** A poster outline or sketch identifying the main content, supporting evidence, and sources for each section.

## 3. Design Your Interactive Element (20 minutes)

{:.mt-4}
Now design a five-minute visitor experience that helps people engage with the research presented on your poster. Create a rough sketch, mockup, or demonstration of the interaction. Don't worry about polish yet. Focus on making the experience concrete and achievable.

**Start with the takeaway:**

1. Before visiting our exhibit, people might assume...
2. After visiting our exhibit, we want them to understand or reconsider...
3. Our interactive element will help visitors explore this idea by...

**Storyboard the experience:**

**1. HOOK. Capture attention**<br>What question, scenario, image, statistic, or example will engage visitors?

**2. INTERACT. Explore the issue**<br>What will visitors actually do? Possibilities include:
- Compare two examples or outcomes.
- Make a decision involving competing values.
- Explore a map, diagram, or dataset.
- Predict an outcome and examine what actually happens.
- Evaluate evidence or competing claims.
- Consider how changing a system's design might change its consequences.

**3. CONNECT. Return to the research**<br>How does the activity connect to the argument, evidence, or broader societal issues presented on your poster?

**4. REFLECT. Leave visitors thinking**<br>What question or insight should visitors take away?

**Required connection:**

> Complete this sentence:
> 
> "Our interactive element helps visitors understand __________, which we explain and support with evidence in the __________ section of our poster."

## 4. Peer Critique (15 minutes)

{:.mt-4}
Exchange your poster outline and interactive concept with another team.

Consider the following questions:

**Research and Analysis**
1. What larger argument does the poster communicate?
2. Does it explain the system and the human and institutional choices shaping it?
3. What evidence supports the argument? What evidence is missing?
4. Does it acknowledge important trade-offs, uncertainties, or competing perspectives?

**Visitor Experience**
5. What will visitors actually do?
6. Does the interactive element help visitors understand or critically examine the larger issue, or is it primarily entertaining?
7. How could the connection between the poster and the interaction be stronger?

Provide specific, constructive feedback.

{% collapsible closed %}
### 5. Revise and Commit to a Plan (10 minutes)

{:.mt-4}
Based on your discussion and feedback, agree on the following:

- **Poster:** What sections, visuals, evidence, and references need to be developed?
- **Interaction:** What exactly will visitors do, and what materials are needed?
- **Research:** What gaps or unanswered questions remain?
- **Logistics:** What equipment, printing, or supplies will you need?
- **Responsibilities:** Who is responsible for creating each component?
- **Thursday milestone:** What will be completed and ready for review by November 5?
