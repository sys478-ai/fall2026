## 1. Discussion: Readings

### 1.1. Risky Business: Advanced AI Companies' Race for Revenue (Required)

#### Discussion Questions

1. The brief describes seven ways AI companies can make money, from subscriptions to ads to government contracts. Pick one. What does the company need from users under that model, and how might that change what the product does?
2. Which claims in the brief are descriptive (what companies are doing), which are normative (what they should do), and which are political / institutional (who has power to change it)? Where is the evidence strongest, and where is it speculation?
3. Anthropic and OpenAI are public benefit corporations, yet the brief argues corporate structure has not insulated them from revenue pressure. What would it take for a company's stated mission to actually constrain its business model?

### 1.2. It's Not Just the Content, It's the Business Model (Optional)

#### Discussion Questions

1. Maréchal and Biddle argue that regulators focus on what users post when they should focus on how platforms amplify and target it. What does each approach reveal, and what does it obscure?
2. The report describes social media algorithms that optimize for predicted engagement. How is an AI chatbot's incentive similar to or different from a feed's? Use the psychological lens: what human tendencies does each business model rely on?
3. The authors recommend transparency about what algorithms optimize for and what variables they weigh. Would that change anything for an ordinary user? Who would have to act on that information for it to matter?
