Today, we'll reflect on the symposium, appreciate some of the ways technology enriches our lives, and imagine alternative technological futures.

## 1. Symposium Debrief (10 minutes)

{:.mt-4}
Let's start by reflecting on the symposium!

- What went well? What are you proud of?
- What surprised you about presenting your work?
- What questions or reactions did visitors have?
- What did you learn from explaining your research to others?

## 2. Technology Appreciation (10 minutes)

{:.mt-4}
We've spent much of this semester critically examining technologies and their consequences (which of course is extremely important). But technology also makes many things possible that we value. 

Think about a technology – big or small, sophisticated or mundane – that has made your life better. Discuss with your table:

- What does this technology allow you to do, experience, or accomplish?
- What do you appreciate about it?
- How might your life be different without it? Or, what did the world look like before the technology existed?

We'll share a few examples with the class.

## 3. Small-group activity (35–40 minutes)


{:.mt-4}
Throughout the semester, we've critically examined emerging technologies – how they work, who develops and deploys them, whose interests they serve, and what consequences they might have.

Today, we're going to shift our focus from critique to possibility. We are going to do this by exploring alternative technologies, simpler or low-tech approaches, and changes to institutions, policies, or social practices that could help us create more desirable futures.

### Part 1: Return to Your Symposium Teams (10 minutes)

Revisit the technology or context your team investigated.

1. **Whose interests does it serve?** Consider its stated purposes and the interests of the companies, institutions, and people involved.
2. **What broader human needs or values are at stake?** For example, creativity, connection, safety, learning, autonomy, well-being, or justice.
3. **Where are the tensions?** Whose needs or interests are prioritized, and whose might be overlooked or harmed?
4. **What other approaches might support these same human needs or values?** Identify **one alternative technology** and **one low-tech or non-technological solution**. What might each approach make possible?

### Part 2: Mixed-Group Discussion (20 minutes)

Form new groups with one representative from each symposium team.

**Round 1: Share Your Projects (8 minutes)**

Each person briefly shares:

- The technology their team investigated and the human needs or values at stake.
- One important tension or concern.
- An alternative technology and a low-tech or non-technological solution that might support similar needs or values.

**Round 2: Imagine Alternative Futures (12 minutes)**

Choose one or two of the human needs or values identified across your projects.

1. **What would a future that better supports these needs or values look like?**
2. **What might help us get there?** Consider existing or new technologies, low-tech approaches, policies, institutions, public investments, or community initiatives.
3. **What possibilities and trade-offs might these approaches introduce?** Who might benefit, and who might be disadvantaged?
4. **Who could help make this future possible?** What decisions or actions could move us in that direction?

### Part 3: Return to Your Symposium Teams (5–10 minutes)

Share one new idea or perspective from your mixed-group discussion.

Together, complete these statements:

- **The future we envision:** We want a world in which…
- **What could make it possible:** This might require…
- **Where we could begin:** One concrete step could be…

Be prepared to share your vision with the class.
