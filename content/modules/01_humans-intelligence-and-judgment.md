We will begin by looking at why AI is already a public and ethical issue: what these systems are being built to do, where they are being used, and how they are shaping decisions in everyday and institutional life.

That said, before asking how machines learn, it is useful to first ask how *people* learn. To do his, we will explore the biological, psychological, social, cultural, and political dimensions of learning. These ideas will give us a foundation for thinking about what shapes human learning, and what may make human intelligence distinctive and difficult to reduce to computation.

The unit then turns to judgment, ethics, and theory of mind: what a system can imitate convincingly, and what it might still lack. We close by asking what futures technologies promise, and who gets to define a desirable one. By the end of this unit, you should be able to explain how claims about machine intelligence depend on assumptions about learning, judgment, and the kinds of futures we consider worth building.
