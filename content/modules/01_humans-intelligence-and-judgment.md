This unit is your conceptual foundation for the course. Instead of starting with models or algorithms, you will ask why AI is a public and ethical issue, and build a shared vocabulary for human learning, judgment, and intelligence before we turn to technical mechanisms.

Across these first meetings, you will compare neuroscientific and psychological accounts of how you learn, ask what makes human intelligence distinctive, and examine judgment, ethics, and theory of mind as capacities machines may imitate without possessing. We close the unit by asking who gets to imagine and define desirable technological futures.

AI is never just a technical artifact. It is shaped by assumptions about intelligence, learning, and the futures we consider worth building. By the end of this unit, you should be able to explain why those assumptions matter before we ever get to datasets, models, or algorithms.

Fall 2026 dates: `Tue, Aug 18 - Thu, Sep 3`

Meeting sequence:

1. `Why Does AI Matter as a Public and Ethical Issue?`
2. `How Do Humans Learn? Neuroscience and Neurobiology`
3. `How Do Humans Learn? Psychological Foundations`
4. `What Makes Human Intelligence Distinctive?`
5. `Judgment, Ethics, and Theory of Mind`
6. `Imagining Futures: Who Gets to Define the Desirable?`

By the end of this unit, you should have a stronger sense of what you bring to learning and judgment, what assumptions sit behind familiar claims about machine intelligence, and what questions you will need as we turn to technical systems.
